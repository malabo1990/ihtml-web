IHTML 1.1 — element connections, meshes with bones and hair with physics

New IHTML editor version 1.1. Elements connect to each other without code, sliders drive the timeline, video and sound, meshes bend with several bones and hair falls with physics in one click. More stable and more secure.

Today, 20 November 2026, we are releasing IHTML 1.1. It is the biggest update since the first version: it makes the editor more interactive, more expressive for character animation and more stable for everyday work.

If you already have IHTML installed on your computer, the app will let you know with a red dot in Settings and an "Update now" button. If not, download it for free from Download.

Connections: elements talk to each other

The headline feature. In the Actions tab of any element there is a new section, Connections, to link one element to another without writing code:

  • A slider moves the timeline, the volume of a video or the rotation of a box.
  • A number or text field shows and changes a value of another element.
  • A checkbox shows or hides something, or plays and pauses an animation.
  • A select changes colours, texts or images.

It works both ways: if the target changes on its own (an animation that plays, a video that runs, a variable), the control updates by itself. And any element can be an input and an output: the position of a box you drag can rotate another one, write a text or store a variable.

Connecting a slider to an animation's Time pauses it at the exact point of the slider: it only moves when you move the slider.

Slider, timeline, video and sound

  • Actions that work in percent: setting a slider to 50 % takes the animation or the video to the middle.
  • New trigger "While an animation plays" and value "Time of an animation".
  • New volume, speed and mute actions for video, audio and YouTube.
  • "Link to scroll" on a slider links the animation to the slider.
  • The time of a video or a YouTube video can also be used as a value.

Mesh, bones and hair with physics

To animate characters like in Spine, Rive or Photoshop's Puppet Warp:

  • Automatic mesh that follows the drawing's outline, with points you add or remove where it needs to bend.
  • Pins (Puppet Warp style): pin a few points, drag another one and the rest bends naturally.
  • Paint weights with a brush to decide how much each bone moves each area.
  • A mesh can now be bound to several bones at once: pick them from a list, by chains, and each strand follows its own chain without pulling its neighbour.
  • Automatic hair chain: one click on a hair image creates the bones along the curve of the strand, turns on physics (Hair, Tail, Rope, Spring or Flag) and binds the mesh. Repeat it for each strand.
  • Improved hair gravity: hair really falls and sways, with a whip effect.

Responsive design

  • Fluid height on Desktop: besides the width, the exported page can fill the whole height of the window. With top/bottom anchors, elements follow that height. With fluid width and height, your project fills the whole screen like an app.

More stable

  • Grouping, ungrouping or moving an animated element to another layer no longer makes it jump.
  • Changing the source of a YouTube video keeps its options (no controls, loop…).
  • Many actions fixed after reviewing 64 element types: "Show", "Change text", colours and focus now work the same in the editor and in the exported HTML.
  • YouTube in the desktop app: automatic reconnection and queued commands.
  • The timeline's layer column has its own width.

More secure

  • Signed automatic updates: the app only installs versions signed by IHTML.
  • Stronger Pro license protection: each license is tied to your devices.

Thanks to everyone who has tried IHTML and sent ideas: many of these new features came from your requests.
